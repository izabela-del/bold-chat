# Bold AI Chat — Design Context

Design guidance for **June**, Bold's AI chat assistant. This is the "why" and the
rules behind how June should talk and behave in chat — a reference for iterating on
the agent and a checklist for reviewing changes.

**Scope:** applies primarily to the **live-Claude June** shown in
`bold-onboarding-june.html`, whose replies are generated by Claude following the
system prompt in `api/june.js`. The same principles apply to the deterministic
copy/widgets in `june-web.html`.

---

## TL;DR — the one rule

**The UI should do the work, not the user.** Our members are adults 65+; reading a
paragraph and then typing a reply is the highest-effort interaction we can ask for.
Every turn should be **short**, ask **one thing**, and — wherever the answer is a
known set — be **tappable** instead of typed.

**Takeaway:** most of the "long copy / too many questions" fixes are **system-prompt**
edits. "Bold text" and "quick-reply chips in the live chat" also need a **small client
change** (see below).

## Issues & guidance (priority order)

1. **One question per turn — never bundle.**
   "What's your mailing address *and* best phone number?" forces two answers and
   usually gets one. Ask one thing, wait, then ask the next.

2. **Shorten copy — lead with the ask.**
   The welcome and the "why Bold" answers run 4–6 sentences. Cut to ~1–2 short
   sentences (or two tiny bubbles). Put the question first; let chips carry the details.

3. **Offer quick-replies for known answers.**
   Conditions, yes/no confirms, state, cost/eligibility topics — anytime the answer is
   a known set, offer buttons. **Conditions especially should be multi-select chips,**
   not open text (typing "sleep apnea" on a phone at 70 is real friction).

4. **Formatting: spacing, bold, small bubbles.**
   Break each idea into its own short bubble (blank line between). **Bold the single
   most important phrase.** Never a wall of text.

5. **Be conversational — ask back.**
   Instead of a monologue on "why Bold is different," answer in one line and turn it
   into a question about *their* motivation. People engage when it's about them.

6. **Defer what's not needed now.**
   June already says the Bold team follows up — so don't collect the mailing address
   in-chat at all. Gather only what's needed to book; note the rest for the human follow-up.

## Message formatting rules (add these to the system prompt)

Drop into the "HOW TO WRITE" section of `systemPrompt()` in `api/june.js`:

- **One question per message.** Never combine two asks in one turn.
- **Keep each bubble to ~1–2 short sentences (≤ ~35 words).**
- **Put each distinct idea or question in its own paragraph, separated by a blank
  line** — the client renders each paragraph as its own bubble, so this is how you get
  short, broken-up messages instead of one block.
- **Bold the single most important phrase** with `**…**` (a number, a "Yes/No", the key
  benefit). Never bold whole sentences.
- **Lead with the answer or the question**, then the short "why" — not the other way around.
- **Prefer a question back over a monologue.** Mirror the member's own words.
- **Offer tappable options** whenever the answer is a known set (see chips convention below).
- Reading level: plain and warm, ~6th–8th grade. No emojis (already in the prompt).

## Concrete rewrites (before → after)

**Welcome** (long list → question + chips)
> **Before:** "Hi Carol — welcome to Bold! I'm June, your Care Coordinator… I can share
> how our Weight Management program works, talk through GLP-1 options and what's covered,
> walk through the cost for your insurance, and gather what we need… To get us started —
> what's on your mind about managing your weight, or where would you like to begin?"
>
> **After:** "Hi Carol — I'm June, your Bold assistant. What brings you in today?"
> `[Lose weight]` `[GLP-1 options]` `[What will it cost?]` `[Just looking]`

**"How is Bold different?"** (short + ask back)
> "Most programs hand you a plan and disappear. At Bold, a real provider builds yours and
> **stays with you** — 45-minute visits and regular check-ins.
>
> What's tripped you up before — the plan, the cost, or sticking with it?"

**Health conditions** (multi-select chips, not open text)
> "Anything your provider should know about? Tap any that apply."
> `[High blood pressure]` `[Diabetes]` `[Heart condition]` `[Sleep apnea]` `[None]` `[Something else]`

**Contact** (one at a time; drop the address in-chat)
> "Last thing — what's the best **number** for the team to reach you?"

**Confirm what's on file** (tighten to a tap)
> "Quick check — born **4/12/1958**, in **California**?" `[Yes]` `[Fix that]`

## Quick-reply chips in the live chat (small feature to add)

Today the live agent returns plain text, so it can't offer chips (that's why the
conditions question is open text). To fix, add a simple convention:

- **Prompt:** when the answer is a known set, end the turn with a machine-readable line,
  e.g. `\n[[chips: High blood pressure | Diabetes | Heart condition | Sleep apnea | None | Something else]]`
- **Client:** parse that trailing line out of the reply, render the options as tappable
  pills, and send the tapped value back as the member's next message. (The deterministic
  flow already has `askChips` — reuse the same pill styles.)

Support a `multi` flag for multi-select questions like conditions.

---

## Bold / rich text (small client change)

`addBotBubble` currently uses `textContent`, so `**bold**` would show literal asterisks.
To honor the "bold key phrase" rule, render a **safe** subset of markdown in the client:
escape HTML first, then convert `**…**` → `<strong>…</strong>`. Pair it with the
system-prompt bold rule above. Keep it to bold only (no links/images) to stay safe.

---

## Identity & compliance note

Keep June's identity consistent and honest: **June is Bold's AI assistant** — not a
"Care Coordinator." "Care Coordinator" is the **human** on the team June hands off to.
The system prompt already says June is an AI and "never implies she is a provider" —
but some copy/screenshots still say "I'm June, your Care Coordinator," which can read as
*a human clinician* to an older adult. Align every surface (live prompt, canned copy,
landing footer) on: *"June, Bold's AI assistant, here to help — a real person on your
care team is always available."*
